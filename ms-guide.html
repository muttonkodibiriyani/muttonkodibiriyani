Writing a strong investment brief

A great brief reads like a one-page board paper. It is concrete, quantified, and falsifiable. Reviewers should be able to disagree with you on the merits within 5 minutes.

  • Problem first, solution second. Open with the customer or commercial pain in numbers — never with the technology.
  • Evidence over opinion. Cite POS data, NPS surveys, time-and-motion studies, or comparable deployments.
  • One primary metric. If you cannot name the single number that will move, you do not yet have a measurable initiative.
  • Be honest about risk. The bear case is your friend — it earns trust with the Committee.
  • Stage your ask. Request Discovery first, not full rollout funding.

The 5-dimension scoring model

Strategy Reviewers (Gatekeepers) score every initiative on 5 weighted dimensions. The score determines whether it reaches the Investment Committee.

DimensionWeightWhat "great" looks like
Financial Return Credibility30%Quantified annual benefit with named source. Payback ≤ 36 months. Sensitivity analysis (base/bull/bear) included.
Customer & Market Validity25%Tier 1 evidence (POS, transactional, surveyed). Comparable deployments cited. NPS / satisfaction baseline.
Technical & Delivery Credibility20%Architecture reviewed. Vendor / build path chosen with rationale. Integration map. Discovery sprint scoped.
Strategic Alignment & Optionality15%Maps directly to Board-level priority. Creates capability re-usable by other brands or markets.
Operational & Risk Credibility10%Store/ops sign-off. Regulatory (PDPL/DIFC) covered. Brand-partner approvals confirmed in writing.

Thresholds: PASS ≥ 75 CONDITIONAL 60–74 FAIL < 60

The 6-shark Investment Committee panel

The Committee evaluates from 6 perspectives plus a Red Team risk modifier. Every initiative is stress-tested by all 7 voices.

  • 🔪 Margin Surgeon — owns payback, EBITDA isolation, double-counting risk.
  • 👤 Customer Advocate — owns the demand evidence and customer pain.
  • 💻 Tech Realist — owns architecture, vendor risk, integration complexity.
  • 📊 Commercial Strategist — owns Board alignment and competitive optionality.
  • 🏪 Operator — owns store readiness, change management, regulatory sign-off.
  • 📏 Measurement Architect — owns the primary metric, attribution, and accountable owner.
  • 🎯 Red Team — applies a risk modifier (typically -3 to -10) for live execution risks.

Writing a strong bear case narrative

A bear case is a forward-dated, falsifiable story of failure. It is the single most powerful trust-building artefact in your brief.

Format: "It is [18 months from now]. The initiative consumed $X and delivered only Y% of the target. The root cause was…"

Strong bear cases name the specific dependency that failed (vendor API, brand approval, integration scope, adoption rate) — not vague risks like "scope creep".

Measurement plan standards

  • Primary metric: exactly one. Named, with baseline and target values.
  • Leading indicators: what moves first to confirm the initiative is taking hold within 30–60 days.
  • Attribution method: A/B, pre/post test-control, difference-in-differences. State the design.
  • Accountable owner: a named individual (not a team or role).
  • Review cadence: formal reviews at minimum quarterly with named dates.

Stage gate exit criteria

Each stage releases ~4% / 30% / 40% / 26% of the total approved budget. Capital is locked behind exit criteria — explicit, binary, and observable.

StageBudget splitTypical exit criteria
Stage 0 — Discovery4%Architecture reviewed; vendor selected or build path approved; integration map signed.
Stage 1 — Alpha/MVP30%MVP live; primary metric instrumented; leading indicators trending positive.
Stage 2 — Scaled Pilot40%Pilot meets ≥80% of target; ops change management complete; PDPL sign-off.
Stage 3 — Full Rollout26%Pilot exceeds target; full ops readiness; benefits actuals captured.

Financial policy

  • Maximum payback: 36 months. Exceptions require explicit Council vote.
  • Quick Win: ≤ $200K total budget, ≤ 12-month payback.
  • Discovery cap: $75K maximum for Stage 0 exploration.
  • Stage funding: 4 / 30 / 40 / 26 percent split, fixed by policy.

FAQ

Q: My initiative crosses brands — who is the sponsor?
A: The brand whose P&L benefits most. Note revenue and benefit splits in the Franchise Note.

Q: How long does Gatekeeper review take?
A: 5 working days SLA from submission.

Q: Can I edit a submitted initiative?
A: Drafts only. Once submitted, ask the Strategy Reviewer to RETURN it for revision.

Q: Where does the Cosmos master key live?
A: Azure Key Vault. The browser never sees it. APIM injects the key at request time.
